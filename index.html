Find the right buyers in Johor

Use LinkedIn's own search — free, safe, allowed. Don't just hunt “IT Manager”: for ELV, power and CCTV, the budget usually sits with facilities, project and M&E people.

  1. In the top search bar, type IT Manager Johor and press Enter.
  2. Click People in the row of buttons below the search bar.
  3. Click All filters → set Locations (Johor Bahru, Iskandar Puteri, Kulai), Connections = 2nd, Industry (Construction, Real Estate, IT Services, Manufacturing) → Show results.
  4. Run more titles — your best buyers — one at a time (copy below).
  5. Turn on Create search alert so LinkedIn notifies you of new matches.
  6. For each good person: open profile → Connect → Add a note → paste a script from section 3. Cap at ~10–15/day.

Ready-to-post content

Post 3× a week. The first two lines decide who clicks “see more,” so they're built to hook. End every post by replying to comments in the first hour — that's what spreads reach.

Educational — the ELV budget leak

Teach · builds authority

ELV is where most building budgets quietly leak money. Here's why.

"ELV" covers the systems owners underestimate: CCTV, access control, cabling, PA, BMS integration. They get value-engineered late in the project — and that's exactly when it goes wrong:

• Cabling undersized for future bandwidth → rip-and-replace in 3 years
• CCTV specced on price, not coverage → blind spots you find after an incident
• BMS bolted on instead of designed in → systems that don't talk to each other

The cheapest ELV package is almost never the cheapest building.

What's the worst ELV shortcut you've seen come back to bite a project?

Proof — critical power case study

Show results · builds trust

A data centre can survive almost anything — except losing power for 8 milliseconds.

We recently delivered the critical-power layer for a [client type] site using Vertiv/Liebert UPS and precision cooling. The brief was simple to say, hard to do: zero downtime, zero thermal excursions.

What made it work wasn't the brand on the box — it was:
• Right-sizing the load, not over-selling capacity
• Redundancy that matches the actual risk tier
• Commissioning that tests failure, not just switch-on

Planning a server room or data-centre upgrade? The power/cooling design decides everything else. Happy to share what we've learned.

Opinion — cheapest quote, priciest build

Take a stance · builds personality

Unpopular opinion: the "cheapest quote" is the most expensive way to build.

In ICT & ELV tenders, the lowest number wins too often — then the variation orders start:
→ Undersized cabling = future rework
→ No integration plan = systems that don't talk
→ No commissioning discipline = defects at handover

Value engineering isn't "buy cheaper." It's "spend where failure is expensive." A good integrator saves you money in year 3, not just at award.
